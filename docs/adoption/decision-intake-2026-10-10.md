## Executive disposition

Extend the existing MAOS Pharos attention-block capability only: make an item-bound open alternative available for decision choices while preserving distinct authorization, none-fit, and need-context responses. A recommendation, score, or confidence value is informational and never grants permission to install, publish, disclose data, change access, spend money, or execute a consequential action. No universal 90% gate is approved. Any model evaluation remains shadow-first and waits for a user-selected low-risk workload, error-cost bound, permitted data/runtime, and independently calibrated protocol.

Keep deterministic code and existing human authority as the baseline. Compare an open encoder/classifier before a hosted decision service; Jev is a possible pinned hosted comparator, not the selected winner. Laya is a plausible specialized local candidate, not a demonstrated drop-in. No target-workload benchmark, installation, candidate-model inference, paid API request, or adoption decision occurred. No Crush trial or Crush-versus-OMP redundancy determination occurred. Current-session dogfood was exercised but is not ratified or complete; no cycle count or promotion is claimed.

No independent Jev-vs-Laya-vs-trained-encoder controlled benchmark on a target workload exists here.

## Confidence boundary: no universal 90% gate

Jev Choice confidence is documented as c = (pmax − 1/n) / (1 − 1/n). This transforms the output distribution; it is not empirically verified P(correct). For two choices, c=.90 corresponds to pmax=.95; for four, pmax=.925. Score confidence describes distribution concentration around its mode; Noul returns P(yes) without a separate confidence. A high score does not establish correctness, acceptable selective risk, or useful coverage. A one-sided P(yes) threshold cannot express certainty in “no.”

A 90% score, 90% empirical correctness, and 90% automation coverage are distinct quantities. Proper scoring incentives are not calibration certificates. Laya's card reports English Khmer accuracy 0.000 with confidence .952 and act_probability near 1 with AUROC .30 on 396 labeled decisions. Jev's jaggedness docs describe adversarial state influence, option-order effects, arithmetic/counting/date errors, and distraction degradation. “Other” is not established as OOD detection. Even calibrated 90% correctness permits one wrong choice per ten; error cost and authority remain separate.

Evaluation, if later authorized, selects a concrete reversible workload and human-reviewed ground truth; separates training, calibration/threshold selection, and untouched test data; predeclares asymmetric error bounds and coverage; includes no-good-option, language (including Portuguese), rare-label, OOD, adversarial/negation, long/truncated, option-order, and schema-change cases; and reports risk/coverage, calibration, subgroup performance, review load, confidence intervals, latency, cold starts, failures, total cost, privacy, and pinned versions. Shadow recommendations come first. Missing scores/distributions, malformed output, timeouts, unsupported language, invalid schema, and drift abstain/fail closed. Consequential actions retain explicit human/policy authorization regardless of score.
